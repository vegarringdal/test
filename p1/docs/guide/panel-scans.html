Scans

A laser scan has three parts: the point cloud, the places the scanner stood (setups) and the panorama photo taken at each setup. The Scans panel shows all three in the same 3D view as your models, so you can compare what was built with what was designed.

Open the panel

On the ribbon, open the Panel tab and click Scans, or press ALT 693. The panel also opens by itself when a scan is loaded.

Import and load a scan

  1. In the Import Manager, open Import LGSX / E57 and pick a Leica .lgsx or an .e57 file. The options are described under Import LGSX / E57.
  2. Press Import. The scan is converted in your browser into one .tdpp file, kept in the store lgsx-files or e57-files unless you chose another. A big scan takes minutes.
  3. Load it from Model Assets like any model (it is loaded for you when Load after import is ticked).

The points are streamed from the file on disk as you move: only the part you look at is in memory, so a scan of several hundred million points can be opened.

Point cloud

The app draws the points nearest the camera in the finest detail and fills in coarser levels further away, up to a fixed number of points. The header shows how many are drawn now.

  • Show points (ALT 1710) — show or hide all points.
  • Colour / Intensity (ALT 1715) — colour the points from the scanner's photo, or grey by how strongly the laser came back.
  • Size (ALT 1711 / ALT 1712) — the size of a point on screen.
  • Budget (ALT 1713 / ALT 1714) — the most points drawn at once, in millions, over all scans. Higher shows more detail and needs more graphics memory (about 16 bytes per point).
  • Max dist (ALT 1732 / ALT 1733) — points farther from the camera than this many metres are neither loaded nor drawn; 0 means no limit. With a limit, the whole budget is spent within that distance, so what is near gets finer. Not used in orthographic views.
  • Focus (ALT 1737 / ALT 1738) — full detail within this many metres of the camera; beyond it the detail falls off. Use it when the budget runs out and you care most about what is near, for example 10 m indoors. 0 gives even detail over the whole picture. Not used in orthographic views.
  • Cache (ALT 1740 / ALT 1741) — how many points are kept in memory beyond the budget, in percent of it (default 50). They are not drawn, but turning or moving back needs no reload. 0 uses the least memory.
  • Budget counts points in view only (ALT 1739) — points come in blocks, and a large block often reaches outside the picture. Ticked (default), such a block is charged only for the part in view, so the budget gives finer detail on screen; the app then holds somewhat more points than the budget. Unticked, every loaded point counts.
  • Adaptive size (fill gaps up close) (ALT 1716) — grows the points of coarse levels so no gaps show. Off by default.
  • Only the entered setup's points (ALT 1720) — inside a setup, draw only the points that scanner position measured: what its photo actually saw.

Measurements and snapping work on scan points as on models. The Stats window has two rows for scans: the points drawn and held, and the memory they use.

Step into a setup and see the photo

  1. Every setup is an orange sphere in the 3D view. Double-click a sphere, double-click its row in the panel, or select the row and press Enter (ALT 1700). The camera moves to where the scanner stood and the photo appears around you.
  2. Drag to look around; the wheel zooms. The photo gets sharper as you zoom in.
  3. Lower Opacity (ALT 1704 / ALT 1705) to blend the photo with the model and the points. At 100 % you see the photo alone.
  4. Over all / Background (ALT 1706) — the photo covers the model and the points, or shows only where nothing else is drawn.
  5. The arrows (ALT 1703 / ALT 1702) go to the previous and next setup; Exit (ALT 1701) leaves the photo.

Show setup markers (ALT 1707) hides or shows the orange spheres, and Markers (ALT 1735 / ALT 1736) makes them see-through when they are in the way; they can still be double-clicked. A setup without a photo can still be entered: only the camera moves.

Scans and setups

  • The checkbox on a scan's row (ALT 1723) hides or shows its points and spheres. The scan stays loaded.
  • The remove button (ALT 1724) takes the scan out of the scene. It stays in the library.
  • Below each scan are its setups; double-click one to enter it.

Good to know

  • Scans imported before a fix or a format change are not converted again by themselves. Import the source file again to get the corrected or smaller file; the app asks before converting a file it has seen before.
  • A .tdpp file can also be made outside the app with the scan command-line tool and imported as it is.
