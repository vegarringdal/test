User guide · App layout F10

Animation

Show how something is put together, one step at a time: items move into place, appear or fade, the camera follows and callouts explain each step. The model itself is never changed — leave animation mode and everything is back where it was. Numbered markers on each picture match the numbers in the text; click a picture to see it full size.

On this page

App layout · F10

The Animation layout

Press F10 (or pick Animation on the App Layout ribbon) to switch to the animation workspace. It opens the Animation ribbon, puts the Timeline, Storyboard and Overlay Preview under the 3D view and the Animation panel on the right.

  1. Animation ribbon — described group by group below.
  2. Hierarchy — pick the items you want to animate here or in the 3D view.
  3. 3D view — shows the animation while it plays, and the move gizmo while you record.
  4. Timeline and Storyboard — the same animation as clips on a time line, or as a row of picture cards. The third tab, Overlay Preview, shows the callout you are designing.
  5. Animation panel (with the Planner and the Overlay Editor behind it) — every part, moving item and callout, and the settings of whatever you click. The Planner holds the step-by-step plan of a big animation.

How an animation is made

  • Parts are the steps of the story: "Base frame", "Pump", "Motor". The Player lists them as chapters and can stop after each one.
  • Clips are single movements inside a part: one item sliding in, one item appearing. A part can hold several clips that play one after another or together.
  • Actors are the items that move. One actor can be a single pipe or a whole skid.
  • Keys are the positions an item passes through. Between two keys the item moves smoothly.
  • Links chain things together: a part starts after the one before, a clip runs with another. Move the first one and everything linked to it follows.
  • Looks are Set Color inside the animation: from a point in time the whole scene is coloured, ghosted or hidden the way the look says — the rest of the plant ghosted while one item moves, a pipe turning red. A look can fade in from the one before.

Items only move and turn; they are never stretched or resized. Nothing is saved into the model: press Close and every item is back exactly where it was. Animation mode also starts from a clean scene — colours, ghosting, hidden items, items you moved with the Transform ribbon and your clipping are put aside, so an animation looks the same for everyone — and gives them back when you close it.

Ribbon

The Animation ribbon

Commands you need less often sit in small menus — File, Presets, Overlay, Link, New look and Panels. Every entry in them has its own shortcut, shown in its tooltip. On a screen narrower than about 1900 pixels, scroll the ribbon sideways to reach the last groups.

Animation

  1. Animate (ALT 901) — starts editing animations. Press it again to stop (ALT 903 leaves the Player too); every animated item goes back to where it was.
  2. File — New animation (ALT 960), Open… an animation file (*.tdanim.json, ALT 925), Save, Export video… (ALT 1410, see Make a video), and the timelines: an animation can hold several, for example "Assembly" and "Maintenance" — pick the one you work on (ALT 924 goes to the next), New timeline (ALT 1349) or Delete this timeline (ALT 1350).
  3. Save (ALT 926) — downloads the animation as a file, including the look of its callouts. It reads Save* when there are unsaved changes.
  4. Panels — opens the Animation (ALT 691), Timeline (ALT 692), Storyboard (ALT 693), Player (ALT 694), Overlay Editor (ALT 695), Overlay Preview (ALT 696), Set Color (animation) (ALT 697) and Planner (ALT 698) panels.

Your work is also kept automatically in the browser. If the app is closed before you save, the Animation panel offers to recover it the next time.

Playing sits in the Player panel (Panels → Player, ALT 694); its shortcuts work anywhere while animation mode is on: play / pause / continue K (or a short tap on SPACE — holding SPACE still moves the camera as before), previous / next part SHIFT+J / SHIFT+L, stop ALT 904, loop ALT 909, faster / slower ALT 910 / ALT 911.

Create

  1. Animate sel. (ALT 930) — makes a new clip for the selected items at the playhead and starts recording: every drag of the move gizmo becomes a key. See Record a movement.
  2. Pull out (ALT 931) — records the item being taken out of its place, which is usually easier, and then plays it backwards so it moves into place.
  3. Part after (ALT 932) — adds a new, empty part that starts where the current one ends.
  4. Wizard… (ALT 933) — builds a whole assembly at once: one part per selected item, in order. See Build an assembly with the wizard.
  5. Presets — ready-made animation from the playhead: movements for the selection (slide in ALT 934, drop in, lift & swing, rotate, spin, appear, and Explode), a Flash (ALT 1404, the selection blinks the Looks key colour) or Pulse (ALT 1405, it glows in and out), a Camera orbit (ALT 1406, one turn around what the view looks at) and a Camera cut (ALT 1407, a camera key that jumps instead of gliding).
  6. Per item (ALT 1333, on by default) — with several items selected, recording and presets give each item its own clip, all moved by the same drags, so you can change any one of them later. Switch it off when the items must move as one rigid block.
  7. Overlay — every overlay in your library: a Callout (ALT 942) — a label with a leader line, pinned to the item selected in the 3D view (or the selected clip's item) and shown while its clip plays — a Caption bar (ALT 1345), a Title card (ALT 1346), an Info panel with text and an image (ALT 1347), a Step card (ALT 1348), and any component you made in the Overlay Editor. It is shown for the selected clip, or from the playhead for 3 seconds; drag it where you want it and change its text in its settings.

Keys

  1. Add key (ALT 943) — adds a key at the playhead that keeps the item where it is now.
  2. Delete key (ALT 944) — deletes the selected keys (click a diamond on the Timeline to select it).
  3. Auto-key (ALT 947) — while on, every drag of the move gizmo writes a key at the playhead for the selected item.
  4. Prev key (ALT 945) — moves the playhead to the previous key.
  5. Next key (ALT 946) — moves the playhead to the next key.
  6. Camera key (ALT 948) — stores the current view at the playhead, so the camera moves there while the animation plays.
  7. Clipping key (ALT 1341) — stores the clip box, planes and shapes on screen at the playhead. See Cut the model open during the animation.
  8. Copy keys (ALT 1402) — copies the keys selected on the Timeline (also on a diamond's right-click menu).
  9. Paste keys (ALT 1403) — pastes them at the playhead into the selected clip, keeping their spacing and easing. Pasted onto another item's clip, a movement becomes the same movement for that item.

Edit

  1. Undo (CTRL+Z) — undoes the last animation change.
  2. Redo (CTRL+Y) — redoes it.
  3. Group (ALT 955) — puts the selected clips together into a new part.
  4. Reverse (ALT 949) — plays the selected clip, or the selected part with everything in it, backwards. Animate the items moving away from where they belong, then reverse the part so they move into place. Reversed clips are striped on the Timeline.
  5. Bake reverse (ALT 950) — rewrites the clip's keys the other way round. It looks the same, but the clip now plays forwards — handy before you edit it.
  6. Link (ALT 951) — links the selected clip or part to the one before it: after it (ALT 951), with it (ALT 952), ends with it (ALT 953), or unlink (ALT 954).

Looks

  1. New look — adds a look at the playhead and opens it in the Set Color (animation) panel: an Empty look (ALT 1322), or a Look from the Set Color panel (ALT 1323) — a copy of what the normal Set Color panel has now, so you can build the colours there as usual and capture them.
  2. Edit look (ALT 1324) — opens the selected look clip in the Set Color (animation) panel. Double-clicking a look on the Timeline does the same.
  3. Spotlight (ALT 1325) — turns everything except the items the focused part moves into a faint white ghost (5 % opacity) while that part plays; the whole model fades back as the part ends.
  4. Key colour — the colour Colour key uses.
  5. Colour key (ALT 1326) — the items selected in the 3D view (or the selected clip's item) take the key colour from the playhead on, fading in.
  6. Own colour (ALT 1327) — from the playhead on, the selected items fade back to their own colour (or the look's).

Looks sit on their own Looks row of the Timeline and belong to the part they are in, so they move with it. At any moment the latest look is in force; before the first one the scene is clean.

View

  1. Motion paths (ALT 956, also in the Player) — draws the path each moving item of the current part takes, in the 3D view — also while playing and in the Player. Switch it off to watch the animation without them.
  2. Camera track (ALT 957) — when on, the camera follows the camera keys while playing. Turn it off to watch from your own view.
  3. Overlays (ALT 921) — shows or hides the callouts and step cards.

Panels

The animation panels

Animation

  1. Parts — the steps in playing order, with their length. Hover a part to play it ▷, loop it, or play it on its own (everything else stays where it is at the part's start). Drag a part to move it in the order; the links are fixed up for you. Open a part (the arrow) to see its clips.
  2. Actors — the items that move. Actor from selection adds the selected items as a new actor. In an actor's settings, Pivot sets what it turns about: the centre of its items, a fixed point, or the pivot you placed with the Transform ribbon's Set pivot (the crosshair, ALT 1352).

Below them, Sequences lists the movements (a movement can be reused by several clips: select it and press Place at playhead, ALT 1351, with the next items selected), Overlays lists the callouts and step cards, and Problems lists anything that will not play as expected, such as an item that jumps between two parts. Click any row to edit it in the settings at the bottom of the panel: names, timing, links, easing, the part's camera, whether it pauses at its end. Click empty space to get the settings of the whole timeline, where you also choose how recording behaves (new clip length, stepping the playhead after each key). There, + (ALT 1349) next to the timeline's name adds another timeline — another take on the same items, picked in the ribbon — and the bin (ALT 1350) deletes the one you are on.

The counter at the top right (for example 5/4096) shows how many separately moving groups of items the animation uses. You only need to watch it on very large animations.

Timeline

Every part is a coloured bar; under it, each moving item has a row with its clips. Clips that run at the same time on the same item get a row each. The red line is the playhead.

  • Click the ruler to move the playhead, or press ← / → to step.
  • Drag a clip to move it in time. Drop it close to the end of another clip and it links after it; close to its start and it links with it. Hold Alt while dropping to place it without a link.
  • Drag the left or right edge of a clip to make it shorter or longer.
  • Select a clip to see its keys as diamonds underneath; drag a diamond to change when that position is reached. The clip (and its part) grows or shrinks with its last key.
  • Click a diamond to edit that key in the 3D view: the playhead jumps to it, the item is selected, and the key shows as a big yellow cross on the item's path. Drag the gizmo to move the item at that key — the key changes, nothing new is recorded.
  • A part's card (a planner card, or the step card for every part) shows as a bar in the Overlays row. It always spans its part: move or resize the part, not the bar. Click it to edit the card.
  • Looks and colour keys are short blocks: drag the block to move it, drag its end to change how long it fades.
  • Double-click a part (or Focus part, ALT 965) to work on it alone: the rest of the timeline dims, the part's name appears in the bar at the top, and new clips, looks and camera keys go into it. Show all next to the name (ALT 1421, or click the animation's name there) leaves the focus.
  • Ctrl + wheel zooms, Shift + wheel scrolls sideways, dragging over empty space selects several clips.
  • Drag the right edge of the name column to make room for long names; the width is kept for next time.
  • Shift + drag on the ruler marks an in/out range to play just that stretch.
  • Right-click for everything else: play, reverse, duplicate, group, unlink, delete, add a marker. Right-click a diamond to delete that key; right-click a marker to rename it, delete it, or make playback pause there (a pause marker shows ⏸).

Storyboard

The same parts as picture cards. The chip between two cards says how they connect — after +0.3s means "after the previous one, with a 0.3 s pause". Click a chip to change it.

  • Drag a card to change the order.
  • Drop a card on the bottom edge of another to make the two play together. A part that runs at the same time as another shows ‖ with 2 on its picture (and ‖ with on the chip before it); every card shows when it starts, so parallel parts share a start time.
  • + between cards adds a new part there.
  • Thumbnails takes a picture of how each part ends, for its card.
  • Click a card to go to that part, or its ▶ to play it — the animation starts if it was not on.

Planner

A plan for a big animation, written as a list of steps. Each step becomes one part. The plan is saved in the animation file, so you can come back and change it later.

  1. Add selected (ALT 1423) puts the items selected in the Hierarchy or the 3D view into the selected step. Add children (ALT 1424) adds the children of each selected node, one item each. Step (ALT 1422) starts a new step; Steps ▾ makes one step for each selected node (ALT 1489) or each child of them (ALT 1490). The motion and duration next to New items are what newly added items get.
  2. Each item has its own motion (slide in, drop in, lift & swing, explode, rotate, spin, appear or stays), the side it comes from, how far away it starts and how long it takes. The bar above the items sets these for many at once: tick items with their checkbox (or tick them all, ALT 1448, and untick a few), then pick a motion, side, distance or duration in the bar. While nothing is ticked, the bar sets every item of the step. Remove (ALT 1449) drops the ticked items, Split (ALT 1480) makes them a new step right after, Move to step… moves them to another step, and Sort ↑ (ALT 1479) orders the items bottom-up, lowest first. The ⋯ on an item (and on the bar, for many) opens its further options: Moves out (disassembly — it leaves its place instead of arriving), easing, a Wait before its motion, the axis, angle and pivot it turns about (rotate, spin, lift & swing), travel In metres, a Custom… direction and a Highlight colour while it moves. While ⋯ is closed, a small note on the item says which of these it has.
  3. Label… — type a text and a callout with it hangs from the item, from the moment it starts to move until the step ends. Leave it empty for no callout. The + in front of it puts the item's name in; the + in the bar above the list does that for the ticked items, or for all of them while none is ticked (ALT 1461). Label text, under the list, makes the step's labels bigger or smaller (ALT 1446 / ALT 1447), the colour next to it colours their leader lines, and Labels stay (ALT 1478) keeps them until the end of the plan.
  4. Generate (ALT 1425) builds the plan into parts on the Timeline.
  • From the second step on, a step starts After the step before (with a Gap) or With it — both play at the same time, the Offset (ALT 1474 / ALT 1475) delays it a little. The picker next to it links to an earlier step instead of the one just before. Inside a step, the items move one after another, all together or staggered. Speed (ALT 1476 / ALT 1477) plays the step faster or slower.
  • A badge on each step shows its state: new (not built yet), changed (it will be rebuilt) or built. Generate only rebuilds new and changed steps. Parts you edited by hand on the Timeline stay as they are, as long as their step in the plan is unchanged. Renaming a step only renames its part.
  • Per step: Hidden until its step keeps its items out of sight until the step starts. Spotlight ghosts everything else while it plays. Pause at end makes playback wait. The picker under them says what the step's items do when it ends: stay, hide (taken away — for a step that removes them) or ghost (left faint) (ALT 1473). Card shows a title card, step card, caption, info panel or one of your own overlays for the whole step. Its text comes from the step's Text. Position puts the card in a corner, at an edge or in the centre of the screen (ALT 1443 steps through them); leave it empty for the card's own place. Text next to it scales the card's text (ALT 1444 / ALT 1445).
  • Camera (ALT 1463 steps through the choices): as the part has it (the Planner leaves the part's own view alone), frame the items or orbit the items (from the side you look from when you choose it — From this side takes it again, ALT 1464; an orbit sweeps the degrees you give), this view (Take this view, ALT 1465), a saved viewpoint, or keep the view. Glide is how long the view takes to get there (ALT 1468 / ALT 1469).
  • Look gives a step its own Set Color rules — hide the piping, colour a system — from the step's start on. Build the rules in the Set Color panel and press From Set Color (ALT 1450), or Load… a saved rules file (ALT 1452). To Set Color (ALT 1451) puts the step's rules back in the panel so you can change them and take them again. Fade is how long the look before takes to blend into it. A step without a look keeps the look of the step before. With Spotlight on as well, the spotlight goes on top: the step's items keep the look's colours and everything else turns into the faint white ghost; when the step ends, its look stays.
  • Clipping: Take clipping (ALT 1470) makes the clipping you have now — box, planes, shapes — the step's section cut from its start on; None (ALT 1471) turns clipping off from there; × leaves it as the steps before have it. More holds the step's Chapter (steps with the same chapter in a row are grouped under it in the Player) and presenter Notes (shown in the Player while the step plays, never in the picture).
  • The step's header: its place in the plan (type a number to move it there), ▶ plays it (ALT 1485, once built), the pointer selects its items in the 3D view (ALT 1486), the arrows move it, ⤶ merges the next step into it (ALT 1481), copy, delete. To give many steps the same settings, tick them (or none, for all; All steps, ALT 1488) and press Copy settings on the step to copy from (ALT 1487) — everything but its name, text, items, links, chapter and notes.
  • Picking a built step selects its part on the Timeline, and picking a part selects its step. Undo and redo (ALT 1491 / ALT 1492) work in the Planner even when the animation is not being authored. Thumbnails (ALT 1493) lets Generate take a picture of each step it builds for the Storyboard.
  • An item can be in several steps — installed, taken away, put back. Each step moves it from where the steps before left it. When you change a step, the later steps that use the same items are rebuilt too. Generate keeps clips and labels you added or moved by hand, and asks before it rebuilds a motion you edited.
  • Click a step to select it. Then ALT 1430 / ALT 1431 go to the next and previous step, and ALT 1428 / ALT 1429 move the step earlier or later. The arrows on an item move it within its step or to the step before or after. Fold steps you are done with, or fold them all (ALT 1442).
  • Deleting a step removes its part the next time you press Generate.
  • Plan ▾ saves the plan as a file, loads one, and makes a brief so Claude Code can write a plan for you — see Plan with Claude Code.

Set Color (animation)

The same editor as the Set Color panel — rules, filters, colours, opacity, Reset model / Append only / Hide model — but it edits the selected look instead of colouring the model. Every change is saved into the look at once (CTRL+Z undoes it). The modes work as in Set Color, with the look before standing in for "what is already coloured": Reset model starts from the clean scene, Hide model from everything hidden, and Append only builds on the look before it — hide the whole model in one look, then show one system with an Append only look, and only that system fades in. The clip's settings offer the same three modes.

  1. Fade (ALT 1330 shorter, ALT 1329 longer) — how long the previous look takes to blend into this one. 0 switches at once.
  2. Show (ALT 1328) — moves the playhead to where this look is fully shown, so you see your changes in the 3D view. Run does the same here.
  3. Mode and rules — as in Set Color. Reset model starts the look from the clean scene; Hide model hides everything and the rules show what they match.

Overlay Editor and Overlay Preview

Callouts and step cards are built from a few blocks — boxes, text, images, icons, a progress bar. The Overlay Editor is where you change their look, and the Overlay Preview shows the result live while you work. They are two panels so you can see both at once: in the F10 layout the editor sits on the right and the preview under the 3D view. Opening a component for editing brings both to the front.

  1. Library — your components and themes. New starts one from a preset; a theme holds the shared colours.
  2. Outline — the blocks of the component. Select one to edit it, add blocks, or move them up and down. Under the outline, Block holds the selected block's colours, fonts, borders, spacing and text. Text can contain placeholders like {{part.name}} or {{part.index}} that fill in by themselves while the animation plays; Parameters add your own, such as a title each callout sets for itself.
  3. Preview (ALT 696) — brings the Overlay Preview panel back if you closed it.
  4. Overlay Preview — the component, live. Fit zooms it to fill the preview; 720p, 1080p and 1440p show its real size on a screen that tall. Pick a dark, light or chequered background, and press Effect to replay how it appears. On the right, Sample values and Sample part let you type the real text — a long title, a two-line description — to see how the card copes. Opened from a callout's Edit style…, the preview starts with that callout's own text. Sample values are only for the preview; they are not saved.

Card size, at the top of the editor, sets how wide the whole card is. With Auto the card is as wide as its text plus its padding, never narrower than Min, and longer text wraps at Max. Fixed keeps it at one width, whatever the text. A block's own Width only sizes that block inside the card.

Press Save in the editor to store the component in your library; every callout that uses it changes with it.

Overlays are included when you take a screenshot from the Home ribbon, at the full picture resolution.

Player

The Player is for watching and presenting, without the editing tools. Its list shows the parts as chapters: finished ones get a tick and the current one a bar that fills while it plays. Click a chapter to go to it. Under the transport buttons and the slider you choose what happens at the end of each part (Stop to wait, Play through, or This part only) and what Continue does with the camera after you have looked around. Lock camera while playing keeps the audience from moving the view. The film button at the top makes a video of the animation (ALT 1410, see Make a video); the PDF button makes the step stills (ALT 1494, see Make step stills). Parts with a chapter (set in the Planner) are listed under it, and the current part's presenter notes show under its name — for you, not for the audience.

Step by step

Common tasks

Build an assembly with the wizard

  1. Select the items in the order they are put together, for example in the Hierarchy. Each selected item becomes one part.
  2. Press Wizard… (ALT 933). Step 1 Items lists what you selected in build order 2. Use the arrows 3 to change the order, or × to leave an item out. Choose One part per child of each selected node to make a part of everything under one item instead.
  3. Step 2 · Motion: how each item arrives — Slide in (and from where), Drop in from above, Lift & swing, or simply Appear. Set how far it travels, how long each part takes and the pause between parts. Timing chooses One after another (a part per item), All together (one part, every item at once) or Staggered (one part, each item a moment after the previous). Tick Hidden until its step so items only show up when it is their turn.
  4. Step 3 · Presentation: Spotlight each part ghosts everything except the part's item while it plays (and brings the whole model back at the end), a Step card naming each part, Frame each part so the camera moves to each item, Pause after each part, and Capture thumbnails for the Storyboard.
  5. Press Generate. The parts appear in the Animation panel, the Timeline and the Storyboard. Press K to play it.
  6. Fix only what is not right: drag a part to another place in the order, change a part's timing in its settings, or re-record one movement.

Plan a big animation

The wizard builds one batch of parts in a single go. For an animation with many steps, use the Planner. It keeps the whole plan, and you can change it and build it again as often as you like.

  1. Open the Planner tab next to the Animation panel (or press ALT 698).
  2. Select the items for the first step and press Add selected. Choose a motion for each item, or set many at once with the bar above the list. Give an item a Label to point it out with a callout.
  3. Press Step for the next step and add its items the same way. Name each step, because the name becomes the part's name everywhere.
  4. Set how each step starts: after the previous one, or with it to run in parallel. Add a Card where the viewer needs an explanation.
  5. Press Generate. Check the result in the Player or on the Timeline, then change the plan and press Generate again. Only the steps you changed are rebuilt.

Plan with Claude Code

For a plan with hundreds of items, let Claude Code write it. The Planner's Plan ▾ menu gives you three files to hand over: the brief (how to build big plans — what to ask you, every option, patterns, your cards and a schema to check against), an item list of the parts you mean, and — to change a plan you already have — the saved plan.

  1. Plan ▾ → Brief for Claude… (ALT 1459) downloads plan-brief.md. It does not depend on what is selected; make it again when your cards change.
  2. To let Claude group by more than names and position — by system, area or discipline — first run a query in the SQL editor whose result has a fullname column and the attributes you care about.
  3. Select the parts in the Hierarchy or the 3D view and pick an Item list for Claude: selected nodes (ALT 1460), children of selected (ALT 1462) or every leaf under the selection (ALT 1495). You get plan-items.csv: each part's name, fullname, parent, depth in the tree, position and size, and the SQL result's columns joined on.
  4. To change an existing plan, also Save plan… (ALT 1456).
  5. Give the files to Claude Code and say what you want, for example: "Build a plan from plan-brief.md and plan-items.csv: bottom-up, one step per level, frame and spotlight every step, a step card at the top right, labels on the motors, the piping grouped by system." The brief tells Claude to ask you for what it is missing.
  6. Back in the Planner, Plan ▾ → Load plan… (ALT 1457) and pick the file Claude wrote. Its steps replace the plan (you are asked first); Append steps… (ALT 1458) adds them after the plan instead. A file with mistakes is not loaded: every mistake is listed with where it is. What loads but is changed — an unknown field, a value out of range, an item twice in a step, a card that does not exist, items not in the loaded models — is listed after loading.
  7. Check the steps, change what you like, and press Generate.

A saved plan keeps the ids of its steps: loaded back over the same animation, only the steps that changed are rebuilt — the brief tells Claude to keep the ids when it edits one. In a copy of the app's source, npm run plan:check plan.tdplan.json checks a file the way Load plan does.

Record a movement

  1. Press Animate if you are not editing already, and move the playhead to where the movement should start.
  2. Select the item and press Animate sel. (ALT 930). A red frame shows you are recording.
  3. Drag the move gizmo arrows and rings to where the item should be. Each drag becomes a key; the playhead steps forward after each one, so drag again for the next position. With Per item on and several items selected, they move together but each gets its own clip.
  4. Press Done on the red banner (or ENTER) when you are done, or Cancel (ESC) to throw the recording away. Add key (ALT 943) holds the current position at the playhead — in every item's clip while recording.

Taking something out is usually easier than putting it in. Use Pull out (ALT 931) instead: drag the item away from its place, press ENTER, and the clip plays backwards so the item moves into place.

Bend a movement

  1. Select the clip and set Path to Smooth in its settings (ALT 1337). The item now moves on a curve through its keys instead of straight lines.
  2. To bend a straight move, put the playhead half-way, press Add key (ALT 943), click the new key's diamond and drag the gizmo sideways. The path in the 3D view shows the curve as you drag.
  3. Keys at the same place are a pause — the item stays still there, even on a smooth path.

Put parts in order

  1. Press Part after (ALT 932) to add a step after the current one, then record or add a template into it.
  2. To change the order, drag a part in the Animation panel's Parts list or a card on the Storyboard. Everything linked after it moves along.
  3. To make two parts play at the same time, drop one card on the bottom edge of the other on the Storyboard, or select the part and press ALT 952 (with).
  4. Untick Pause at end in a part's settings if the next part should follow without waiting.

Explain a step with a callout

  1. Select the item in the 3D view and pick Overlay → Callout (or press ALT 942). A label with a leader line appears next to it while its clip plays.
  2. Click the callout in the Animation panel's Overlays list to change its text, where it points and when it shows and hides. Text size (ALT 1395 / ALT 1396) makes its text bigger or smaller without changing the design; Scale grows the whole card. With Leader line on, Leader colour sets the colour of the line and dot that tie it to the item.
  3. To delete a callout, press the bin on its row in the Overlays list, right-click its bar on the Timeline and pick Delete overlay, or press ALT 1388 while it is selected. Ctrl+Z brings it back.
  4. To change how all callouts look, open the Overlay Editor; the Overlay Preview shows each change as you make it.

Moving a callout over time — for example to keep it clear of the item while the camera turns: in its settings press Key here (ALT 1338), move the playhead, and drag the callout in the 3D view. Every drag at a new playhead time adds a position key (diamonds on its Timeline row); in between it glides. Clear (ALT 1339) gives it one fixed place again.

Labels attached to an item never leave the view: when the camera moves so a label would be cut off, it jumps to the other side of its leader line or is pushed back inside. Switch this off under the timeline settings (Keep overlays on screen, ALT 1340) if you place them by hand.

Move the camera with the story

  1. Move the playhead to where the camera should be somewhere, set up the view, and press Camera key (ALT 948). Repeat for the next view; the camera glides between them. To jump instead, use Presets → Camera cut (ALT 1407), or select a camera key and set Arrive to Cut (ALT 1409; Glide ALT 1408).
  2. For a turntable, frame the item, move the playhead and pick Presets → Camera orbit (ALT 1406): the camera turns once around it in 8 seconds. Change the clip's speed to go faster or slower.
  3. Or give each part an entry view: open the part's settings and use the current view as this part's entry camera. Pressing Next part then also brings the camera there.

With Camera track on, the camera follows the camera keys while the animation plays and while you drag the playhead. As soon as you move the view yourself — mouse, touch, the view cube, or a button such as Fit, Home or a viewpoint — the camera stays where you put it, also while you scrub or play on, so you can set up the next view and press Camera key. R (or Continue in the Player) brings it back to the animation's view; switch Camera track off to keep your own view all the time.

Hide, ghost or colour the plant during the animation

  1. Move the playhead to where the change should happen and focus the part it belongs to.
  2. Press New look (ALT 1322). The Set Color (animation) panel opens on the new look.
  3. Add rules exactly as in Set Color: for example a rule Wildcard * with opacity 0.15 ghosts everything, and a second rule for a system name with a red colour paints it.
  4. Set Fade if the change should blend in, and press Show to see it.
  5. Add another look later on to change the picture again, or an empty look to go back to the clean scene. Set a look to Append only to keep what the look before it shows and only add its own rules — step by step, more of the plant appears.

Already coloured the model the way you want in the normal Set Color panel? Pick New look → Look from the Set Color panel (ALT 1323) instead — the look gets a copy of those rules. For the most common case, ghosting everything except what a step moves, press Spotlight (ALT 1325) on a focused part.

Colour an item while it moves

  1. Select the items in the 3D view (or the animated item's clip on the Timeline, or the item in the Actors list). Several selected items get one colour key together.
  2. Pick the colour next to Colour key and move the playhead to where it should turn that colour.
  3. Press Colour key (ALT 1326). Later, press Own colour (ALT 1327) where it should go back. Both fade over half a second; change that in the clip's settings.
  4. To draw the eye for a moment, pick Presets → Flash (ALT 1404) or Pulse (ALT 1405) instead: the selection blinks or glows in the key colour and goes back to its own colour. The clip's settings change the colour of the whole highlight.

Animate many items at once

There are three ways, depending on what should happen:

  • As one block — switch Per item off, select all the items and use Animate sel. or a template: they move together as one piece (a skid with everything on it).
  • Each its own way, at the same time — keep Per item on (ALT 1333, the default) and record or pick a preset: every item gets its own clip with its own copy of the motion, so each can be changed later. With a preset, Stagger (ALT 1334, in the Presets menu) starts each one a little after the previous for a ripple (twenty bolts dropping in one after another).
  • Exploded view — Presets → Explode (ALT 1335) moves every selected item outward from the middle of the selection.

For a whole assembly, the wizard's Timing does the same: All together or Staggered puts every item in one part. And any two parts or clips can run side by side: drop one card on the bottom edge of another on the Storyboard, or link it with the other (ALT 952).

Cut the model open during the animation

  1. Set up the clipping with the usual ribbons — clip box, clip planes or clip shapes.
  2. Press Clipping key (ALT 1341). The clipping is stored at the playhead on a Clipping row of the Timeline.
  3. Move the playhead, change the clipping (move the box, push a plane further in) and press Clipping key again.
  4. While playing, the box and planes glide from key to key — a section sweeping through the model. Select the Clipping clip and choose Switch if they should jump instead.

Animation mode starts without clipping and gives your own clipping back when you close it. Tick Camera key also keys clipping in the timeline settings (ALT 1342) to store both with one key.

Present an animation

  1. Press ALT+F2 (the Present layout on the App Layout ribbon). You get a large 3D view with the Player beside it.
  2. Press Play (or tap SPACE). With After each part: Stop the animation waits at the end of every step; press Continue when your audience is ready.
  3. While it waits you can look around freely. Continue then glides back to the animation's view, unless you chose Keep my view.
  4. To answer a question about one step, click that chapter, or play it alone with the play button on its row.

Save and share an animation

  1. Press Save (ALT 926). The animation is downloaded as a .tdanim.json file.
  2. Anyone with the same models loaded can open it with Open… (ALT 925) and play it.

Make a video

  1. Pick File → Export video… (ALT 1410) on the Animation ribbon, or the film button in the Player.
  2. Choose the Size (720p, 1080p or 1440p — the width follows the shape of the 3D view), the Frame rate, and What to export: the whole timeline, the focused part, or the in / out range you marked on the Timeline.
  3. Quality: Draft renders each frame once and is quick; Best smooths every frame like a screenshot and takes several times longer. Wait at pausing parts holds the picture at the end of parts that pause there (1 s by default). Untick Overlays to leave out the step cards, callouts and labels.
  4. Press Export (ALT 1411). The video is made frame by frame, so it plays smoothly however slow the computer is; a bar shows how far it is. Keep the tab in front while it runs; Cancel (ALT 1412) stops it. The .mp4 is downloaded at the end.

The camera follows the camera keys. Without any, it glides to each part's entry view. The selection is cleared for the video (it would show highlighted); the playhead and your own view come back afterwards.

Make step stills

For work instructions: File → Export step stills (PDF)… on the Animation ribbon, or the PDF button in the Player (ALT 1494). Every part gets a page: its number and name, a picture of the part's end — everything in place, from the part's own view when it has one, with its overlays — and its description under the picture. The .pdf is downloaded at the end.
